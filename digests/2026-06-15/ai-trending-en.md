### 3. Trend Signal Analysis

**Agent security is the new frontier.** NVIDIA/SkillSpector's explosive +964 debut is the clearest signal: as AI agents gain the ability to execute code, call APIs, and access files, the security surface of "agent skills" has become a first-order concern. This mirrors the broader industry shift from "can we build agents?" to "can we build *safe* agents?" — expect a wave of agent security, sandboxing, and auditing tools.

**The agent harness / orchestration layer is consolidating.** Projects like ECC (⭐215,506), hermes-agent (⭐193,525), and CowAgent (⭐45,298) all compete in the "agent harness" space — providing skills, memory, tool routing, and multi-model abstraction. This layer is becoming as critical as the models themselves, and the community is gravitating toward solutions that work across Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, and OpenCode simultaneously.

**RAG is evolving into persistent agent memory.** The line between RAG and memory is blurring. claude-mem, mem0, and cognee all treat context persistence as a first-class problem, not just document retrieval. The trend is toward systems that learn from every interaction and automatically surface relevant history — a shift from static knowledge bases to dynamic, self-improving memory.

**Domain-specific foundation models continue to proliferate.** Kronos (finance) joins TradingAgents and OpenBB as evidence that vertical LLMs are gaining traction. The era of "one model for everything" is giving way to specialized models fine-tuned on domain corpora, often outperforming general-purpose LLMs on niche tasks.

**Rust is emerging as a serious AI infrastructure language.** With rig, meilisearch, qdrant, and zvec all in Rust, there's a clear signal that performance-critical AI infrastructure is migrating from Python to Rust — driven by the need for throughput, memory safety, and deployment efficiency.
